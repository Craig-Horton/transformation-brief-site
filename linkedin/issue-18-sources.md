## Verified, in window

### Gartner, chief supply chain officers unclear on AI investment returns
- Date: **5th August 2026**
- URL: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-05-gartner-survey-finds-majority-of-chief-supply-chain-officers-unclear-on-ai-investment-returns
- **55%** of chief supply chain officers are unclear on the ROI of their AI investments
- **67%** of supply chain digital investment is now allocated to AI
- Method: 394 supply chain professionals at organisations with revenue of at least $250m, surveyed November 2025 to February 2026; plus 135 senior supply chain leaders surveyed January to April 2026
- Gartner's stated cause: AI use cases proliferate faster than organisations can develop change management approaches to support them
- Gartner's stated fix: a rightsized change management strategy that explicitly links change execution to AI strategy, supply chain strategy and broader enterprise priorities, directing change resources toward outcomes rather than individual activities

### KPMG, Transforming the Enterprise 2026
- Date: **June 2026** (not August, correcting the original sweep)
- URL: https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/transformation/transforming-the-enterprise.html
- PDF: https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/xx/pdf/2026/06/transforming-the-enterprise-2026.pdf
- Organisations manage an average of **3.5 concurrent transformations**
- **48%** describe AI-driven productivity gains as **incremental**, 40% significant, **11% substantial**
- Method: 1,750 senior transformation leaders across 20 countries, plus perspectives from ServiceNow, Workday, SAP and Oracle
- KPMG framing: organisations are scaling AI faster than they are redesigning the enterprise to support it, leaving programmes stuck in localised productivity gains rather than enterprise-wide results

**Correction on the record.** An earlier summary rendered these figures as "48% see massive individual productivity gains, only 11% have achieved enterprise-wide scaling". That is a misreading. The 48% refers to gains described as incremental, and the 11% to gains described as substantial. Use the corrected wording.
